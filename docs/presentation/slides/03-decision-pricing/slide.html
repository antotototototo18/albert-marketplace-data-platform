BENJAMIN, 2 min. BigQuery can be paid in two ways. Like a taxi meter: you pay for the data each query reads. Or like a rent: you reserve computing power, and you pay every hour, used or not. We chose the meter. And we added a safety limit: any query that would read more than 1 GiB, about one gigabyte, is refused before it runs. Why the meter? Because our volume is tiny. We estimate we read about 0.034 TiB a month. A TiB is about a thousand gigabytes. The first TiB is free every month, so today we pay nothing. Renting only becomes cheaper above 258 TiB a month. We are about 7,600 times below that. We looked at two alternatives. Renting without a contract would cost us about 31 dollars a month, because even a tiny query books 50 units of computing power for a full minute. A one-year contract costs 1,927 dollars a month, paid even at night and on weekends. What does our choice cost? A meter has no ceiling. Our 1 GiB limit is set in our own tools. We could not set it on the whole project, because the project belongs to the school and we don't have that right. So a member who forgets the setting is not protected. What would change our mind? Two months above 258 TiB, or one real query blocked by the limit. That would mean the data has grown, and we would measure again. HANDOVER: Anthony takes the second decision: who can read what.
